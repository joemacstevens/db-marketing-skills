On the number. The moment someone says their number, it slams in big: a red DOWN tag, the number in heavy condensed type, and how long it took underneath. The number counts up to its value in about a third of a second, so it lands on the word.

Two weights get a strike. When someone gives a before and after (224 to 184, 185 to 160), the old weight appears first, a red line slashes through it, and the new weight drops in under it.

The weight-loss bar. A thin bar runs across the top and fills every time a number lands: 5, 20, 45, 75, 115, 155, 177. It's full on the last line, and the end card reads 177 LBS DOWN. That total is only what these seven people said on camera, added up.

Different from recent reels. No red name chips or move cards. Big white Oswald, the brand's headline face, with red only as the highlighter. No names on screen, since most of these members are unnamed on tape.

Sound and look

Their own voices, nothing under them. Every bite plays its own audio, levelled so a quiet phone clip matches a loud one. No music under the talking (several clips already have gym music in the background). The anthem hook comes in on the end card.

Cuts. Hard cuts between people to keep it quick. A short push into each before-and-after so the reveal feels like a reveal.

Grade. The house Sample cinematic grade, like every new reel.

Not repeats. The 22-pound man was in the Oct 9 compilation and the 40-pound young man posted Oct 4. Both are outside the two-week window.

Two calls I made

No title card up front. The house rule is 1.5 s of footage, then a 1.5 s title card. Here the title is the last thing he says, so it lands at the end instead and the open stays on Don's face. If you want the card up front anyway, it goes in right after Don's question.

The running total. 177 is the seven spoken numbers added up, nothing estimated. If you'd rather keep each person's number on its own, the bar comes out and the end card is just the title.
